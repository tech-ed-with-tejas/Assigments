 2. Keyword Correspondence
-  The ATS matches the keywords given in the speciﬁc job description with keywords in the candidate’s resume. If your resume lacks enough matching keywords, the ATS will mark it as low priority.

 3. Filtering and Ranking

 Create an ATS-friendly Resume
-  The ATS-friendly resume meaning lies in creating a document that an applicant tracking system can scan, process, and evaluate easily. By intentionally formatting and including relevant keywords, a resume may, among other reasons, survive the ﬁrst cut. This section outlines some practical tips for ensuring visibility and compatibility within an ATS.

 1. Keep it clean and simple
-  Stick to a clear, straightforward layout, no fancy fonts, graphics, or cluttered designs. The heading should highlight “Experience,” “Skills,” and “Education” to ensure the ATS can easily navigate your resume.

 2. Use job-speciﬁc keywords
-  The ATS is designed to scan for speciﬁc keywords mentioned in the job description. Keyword matching is your golden ticket to passing the ATS ﬁlter and landing on the recruiter’s desk.

 3. Avoid fancy fonts and graphics
-  ATS prioritizes content, so readable fonts work best. Select Arial and Calibri to make it more readable by ATS. Skip the graphics, images, and complex formatting that can confuse the software and lead to your resume being rejected.

 4. Save your ﬁle in an appropriate format
-  Save your resume in .docx or PDF format; these are the ﬁle types most ATS systems can read without an issue. Avoid resume formats such as .jpg or .png.

 5. Use clear and speciﬁc job titles
-  Your job titles should be recognizable and aligned with industry standards. For example, if you’re a ‘Project Coordinator,’ avoid getting creative by labeling it as ‘Project Wizard.’ ATS systems scan for speciﬁc keywords, so using clear and direct language ensures your roles are properly recognized.

6. Use bullet points
-  Bullet points make your resume easier to scan for both the ATS and the recruiter- by highlighting your achievements and key qualiﬁcations.

 7. Balance hard skills and soft skills
-  ATS scans for both hard skills (job-related tools) and soft skills (like ‘fast learner’ or ‘communication’). login Register Be sure to highlight your core competencies in both areas- especially those listed in the job description.

 8. Do not overcomplicate the format
-  ATS avoids headers, footers, or columns. Stick to a basic, easy-to-read text format. Your resume should be a seamless experience for the ATS to read, not a puzzle to solve.

 9. Spell out acronyms
-  While abbreviations (like “SEO”) are common, always spell out terms at least once (e.g., “Search Engine Optimization (SEO)”). This ensures the ATS captures both the full form and the abbreviation.

 Common Mistakes to Avoid in an ATS-Friendly login Register Resume

 1. Do not use complex designs or images:
-  Graphics, icons, headshots, charts, or innovative layouts may look great. However, most ATS cannot read graphics or unusual formatting. A complex design or too many images can make your resume unreadable by the system. This might cause it to be rejected before a recruiter ever sees it.

 2. Do not use unusual fonts:
-  Decorative or unusual fonts might seem unique, but they can be a trap. ATS systems parse simple, text-based resumes, and using nonstandard fonts causes errors, misreads information, or even skips important content entirely.

 3. Do not use tables, columns, or text boxes:
-  Tables and columns can interfere with an ATS’s ability to accurately parse your resume. Most ATS tools read content in a top-to-bottom, left-to-right sequence. Unusual layouts or misaligned elements can confuse the system, leading to missed or scrambled information.

 4. Do not put crucial information in headers or footers:
-  Avoid placing contact details in headers or footers, as ATS may miss reading them and fail to pick up your email address or phone number.

 5. Avoid formatting errors and misplaced information:
-  The recruiter will reject your resume if you share it in the wrong ﬁle format. Always send in .docx or plain PDF. Never send .jpg, .png, or any other format that is not ATS-readable.

 6. Do not use buzzwords:
-  Avoid vague terms like “platoon player” or “go-getter” as ATS may overlook them. Rather, make sure to incorporate work-speciﬁc language that images the job.
