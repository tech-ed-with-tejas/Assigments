 Why Optimizing Resume Keywords Matters
-  Most companies use ATS software to screen resumes during the initial selection phase. These systems prioritize certain terms and phrases found in job postings. Missing or misplacing critical keywords can result in your application being overlooked, even when you’re highly qualified.
-  Recruiters and hiring managers expect resumes to reflect the terminology and requirements listed in their job descriptions. By adjusting your resume language to match those needs, you demonstrate direct alignment with the role and improve your chances of being shortlisted.

 Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

 How do I extract the right keywords from a job description?
-  Scan the job post for repeated skills, qualifications, software, and role responsibilities. Build a list of these terms and make sure they are reflected throughout your resume, especially in the summary, skills, and work history.

 Is it better to use exact keywords or synonyms?
-  Whenever possible, use the exact phrases from the job description to maximize ATS matches. Including synonyms or closely related terms is helpful, but prioritize the language used by the employer.

 How often should I tailor my resume for different applications?
-  Tailor your resume for every application, as each role and company may use different wording or prioritize different skills. Tools like CraftedCV allow you to do this instantly with one click for each job posting.

 How do resume scanners and optimizers work?
-  They compare the terms in your resume to those in the job description, detecting missing or poorly placed keywords. They offer suggestions for improvements to improve alignment and ATS scores.

 Should keywords appear only in the skills section?
-  No, keywords should be integrated across the summary, work experience bullet points, and educational background. Strategic distribution across sections signals comprehensive fit to both ATS and recruiters.

 What file format is best for ATS compatibility?
-  The preferred formats for most ATS systems are .docx (Microsoft Word) and plain text. PDF formats sometimes create parsing issues, especially with complex designs or images.
